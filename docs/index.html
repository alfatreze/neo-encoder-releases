About the times. A megapixel is a 1000×1000 picture; most phone photos are 8–12 megapixels, so scale up accordingly. Times were measured on one Mac with two encodes running at once and will differ on your machine. Fast only allows the quickest recipes, up to about 3× the time of the very quickest. How to read these results

Saving
How much smaller the file gets compared with the original. 65% saving means the new file is 35% of the original size.
Quality levels
Visually lossless: differences are essentially invisible. High quality: very hard to spot at normal size. Good for web: fine for most pages, small differences on close inspection.
Effort
How long each image may take to compress. Fast uses only quick settings, Balanced allows moderately slow ones, Thorough tries everything and can take many times longer for a small extra gain. Real times per megapixel are shown beside each level.
Recipe and chain
A recipe is an ordered chain of steps such as palette reduction, OxiPNG repacking and Zopfli squeezing. Order and settings both matter.

Technical note. Quality is judged today with NEO's own SSIM score (8×8 luma blocks, transparent images checked over black and white). The levels above are SSIM ≥ 0.995, ≥ 0.98 and ≥ 0.95. SSIM is a general similarity score but it saturates near the top, so finalists are being re-scored with SSIMULACRA2, a perceptual metric, and the pages will switch to it when that is finished.

How the results are produced

  1. A fixed set of images. Public-licence photos, UI screenshots, icons and pixel art.
  2. Thousands of recipes. Each image goes through every recipe; size, time and quality are recorded.
  3. Best that meets the bar. For each quality level, the smallest result that still passes wins.
  4. Tested on unseen images. Recipes picked on some images are checked on others, to avoid flattering results.
  5. Cross-checked. Finalists are re-scored with a second metric and compared with ImageOptim.
